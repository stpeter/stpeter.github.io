Aristotle's Eudemian Ethics

Book II, Chapter 6

Translated by Peter Saint-Andre

Let us make a fresh start for the ensuing inquiry. All beings are by nature sources of a certain sort, so that each also has the capacity to beget many things of the same kind, as for instance a human begets human beings and in general an animal begets animals and a plant begets plants. On top of this, along among animals a human being is the source of certain actions, for we would not say that other animals take action. This sort of source, from which changes first arise, is called decisive - most fittingly that sort of source from which arise things that cannot be otherwise, as perhaps the god rules. In case of unchanging sources, such as those of mathematics, there is nothing decisive, although they are called sources by a certain likeness; for here too if the source were changed then everything shown would be altered (however, they do not alter themselves if one of them is refuted by another, except by refuting the hypothesis and therefore showing something in that way).

A hunan being is a certain source of things, for an action taken is a change. Just as in other matters the source is responsible for what is or what comes to be, so here we must think about the action as if it were a kind of spelling something out. For if, the triangle having two right angles, the rectangle must have four right angles, plainly what is responsible for this is the fact that the triangle has two right angles. And if the triangle were altered then the rectangle would be altered, too - for instance, if it had three right angles then the rectangle would have six, and if it had four right angles then the rectangle would have eight. And if the triangle were not altered but were to stay as it was, then so too would the rectangle. The necessity of what we are trying to show is clear from the Analytics; right now we can say precisely neither that it is so nor that it is not so, except this far: if nothing else is responsible for the triangle being as it is, then it will be a sort of source and responsible for what follows from that. Thus if it is possible for some things to be the opposite of what they are, then it must also be possible for their sources to be that way (for what flows from things that are necessary is also necessary, whereas what flows from these can turn out in an opposite way). And many things of this sort are up to human beings, and of them human beings are the source. Thus of the actions for which a person is the source and is decisive, it is plain that these can come about or not, and that it is up to oneself what the things over whose being or not being one is decisive in fact come to be or not. And one is responsible for things that up to oneself to do or not to do, and the things for which oneself is responsible are up to onself.

Excellence and deficiency, along with the deeds that flow from them, are praiseworthy and blameworthy respectively (for blame and praise are not assigned due to things that happen by necessity or luck or nature, but due to things for which we ourselves are responsible - and if someone else is responsible they receive the praise or blame). So clearly excellence and deficiency are all about those things for which one is onself responsible and the source of taking action. Thus we must grasp what sort of things it is for which one is responsible and the source of taking action. Now everyone agrees that things which are done willingly and as directed by each person's commitments are things for which one is responsible, whereas thing done unwillingly are not things for which one is responsible. Moreover, it is clear that anything forechosen is done willingly. Thus it is also clear that excellence and deficiency would be among the things that are done willingly.
